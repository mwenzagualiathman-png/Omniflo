![How AI Is Changing Cybersecurity: Benefits, Risks and Practical Protection  Introduction: A New Era of Cybersecurity  Artificial intelligence is changing how people work, communicate and conduct business. But its influence is extending into another important area: cybersecurity.  Cybersecurity traditionally depends on people and software identifying threats, investigating suspicious activity and fixing vulnerabilities. AI is beginning to accelerate these processes.  However, the same technology that helps security professionals defend systems can also be misused by attackers.  Is artificial intelligence making the digital world safer, or introducing new security challenges?  The answer is that it is doing both.  1. How AI Is Strengthening Cybersecurity  One of AI's biggest advantages is its ability to analyse large amounts of information and identify unusual patterns.  In cybersecurity, this can help organisations:  - Detect suspicious activities faster. - Identify potential weaknesses in software. - Investigate security alerts. - Prioritise vulnerabilities that require attention. - Assist developers in preparing security fixes.  A notable development came on November 12, 2026, when OpenAI announced two cybersecurity products: Daybreak Red Team and Daybreak Defense Factory.  These tools are designed to use AI agents to investigate software vulnerabilities and help security teams prepare fixes.  This demonstrates how AI is moving beyond simply answering questions and becoming an active tool in technical security operations.  2. The Growing Risk of AI-Powered Cyberattacks  AI also creates new opportunities for cybercriminals.  Attackers can potentially use AI to improve phishing messages, automate parts of their operations, analyse potential targets and develop more convincing scams.  AI agents introduce another concern. Unlike traditional chatbots, agents can interact with tools, access information and perform tasks.  If they are given excessive permissions or connected to poorly secured systems, they may expose sensitive information or perform actions that were never intended.  This is why security researchers and policymakers are increasingly discussing how autonomous AI systems should be controlled.  3. Why Businesses Should Pay Attention  Cybersecurity is not only a concern for large technology companies.  Small businesses, online shops, freelancers and organisations that store customer information also face digital risks.  A compromised email account, stolen password or exposed customer database can damage customer trust and disrupt business operations.  AI can help improve security, but it should not replace basic protection.  Businesses should prioritise:  - Strong, unique passwords. - Multi-factor authentication. - Regular software updates. - Secure backups of important information. - Careful control of employee and AI-tool permissions. - Verification before sharing sensitive information.  These measures remain important even when advanced AI security tools are available.  4. The Future: Human Expertise and AI Working Together  The future of cybersecurity will increasingly involve cooperation between people and intelligent systems.  AI can help detect patterns, investigate threats and recommend solutions. Human professionals remain essential for reviewing decisions, understanding business risks and controlling sensitive actions.  The goal should not be to give AI unlimited access to digital systems. It should be to use AI responsibly, with clear permissions, monitoring and human oversight.  As AI capabilities grow, responsible security practices will become just as important as technological innovation.  Conclusion: Technology Must Be Useful and Secure  Artificial intelligence is creating powerful opportunities to strengthen cybersecurity, but it is also changing the nature of digital threats.  For individuals and businesses, the lesson is simple: adopting new technology should always come with an understanding of its risks.
